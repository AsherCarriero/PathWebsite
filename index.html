About

What Path is

Path Consulting is an undergraduate organization at Claremont McKenna College. Each semester we assemble teams of students, match each team to a partner organization, and spend the semester producing a research deliverable for that partner. Teams are supervised by the club's leadership throughout.

We work with city governments, regional agencies, school districts, elected officials, and nonprofits. Most of our partners are in Los Angeles County and the San Gabriel Valley, though we have worked with organizations in other states.

Our aim is to increase the capacity of local institutions to respond to community needs, and to give students practical experience with the research and analysis that public work actually requires. Both halves matter. A project that teaches students nothing is not worth running, and a project that produces nothing useful for the partner is not worth their time.

Working with Path

A Path engagement runs the length of an academic semester. We scope the project with you before the semester begins, check in on an agreed schedule while the work is underway, and deliver a finished product before the semester ends. Most partners spend about an hour with us at the start and an hour at the end, plus whatever interim check-ins are useful.

Typical deliverables include research reports, policy memos, comparative studies of how other jurisdictions handled a problem, cost and program evaluations, tracking spreadsheets, and materials intended for a council, a board, or the public.

There is no cost, no contract, and no procurement process. We do not do legal or engineering work and we are not a substitute for licensed professional services. If you are unsure whether something fits, ask. We would rather scope around what your staff is short-handed on than propose a project from the outside.

Fall 2024 Claremont Mayor Pro Tem Corey Calaycay Uncontested elections after district voting reform
AreaElectoral systems

As California cities moved from at-large to district-based voting to comply with the California Voting Rights Act, Claremont wanted to know whether the change was reducing the number of contested races. Mayor Pro Tem Corey Calaycay asked us to look.

We built a comparative dataset of 21 small California cities and tracked election outcomes before and after each city's transition. Contested races fell by more than 20 percent, appointments to vacant seats more than doubled, and canceled elections rose sharply. Smaller candidate pools inside single districts appear to be the mechanism. We delivered the analysis with options for the city to consider on candidate recruitment and filing.

Contested races fell more than 20 percent across the cities studied. Appointments to vacant seats more than doubled.

Fall 2024 Touchstone Mental Health Zoning and community barriers to supportive housing
AreaHousing policy

Local opposition had stalled Touchstone's Lincoln on the Creek supportive housing project in Edina, Minnesota. Touchstone asked us to examine how stigma and zoning restrictions block supportive housing statewide, and what could be done about both.

We produced a comparative analysis of legislation similar to Minnesota's SF-1370, public outreach materials including an op-ed and a brochure, a proposal for a virtual facilities tour to make the buildings legible to neighbors before they are built, and an interview series putting residents' experience in front of the public.

Delivered a legislative comparison and a public engagement package built for use in front of a planning commission.

Fall 2024 Claremont Mayor Sal Medina Tiny homes as transitional housing
AreaHomelessness

Homelessness in Claremont had roughly doubled since 2020 against limited shelter capacity. Mayor Sal Medina asked whether a tiny homes village was a realistic option for the city.

We delivered a research report, a public-facing article, and a funding roadmap to the Mayor and the City Council. The work drew on interviews with unhoused residents, cost analysis, and case studies of programs in Tacoma and Santa Clara. It identified California's Homekey and HHAP programs as the viable funding routes for a Claremont pilot.

Comparable tiny home programs moved up to 40 percent of residents into permanent housing, outperforming traditional shelter.

Spring 2025 Claremont Unified School District, Board President Kathryn Dunn Why a special election cost twice its estimate
AreaElection administration

The district's 2023 special election cost nearly double the original estimate. Board President Kathryn Dunn asked us to find out why, and whether any of it could be recovered.

We examined California election law, county reimbursement mechanisms, and comparable cases in other districts. Special election costs are systematically underestimated at the point of authorization, and the recovery mechanisms that exist are hard for small districts to reach. Our memo set out legislative options to improve cost transparency and reimbursement access. It was shared with Representative Judy Chu's office.

Cost underestimation is structural, not local. Small districts face the highest barriers to recovering funds.

Spring 2025 Los Angeles County Development Authority Continuum of Care and Coordinated Access System evaluation
AreaHousing policy

LACDA asked us to evaluate its Continuum of Care and Coordinated Access System, the infrastructure that moves people from the street into housing across Los Angeles County, with attention to how the agency coordinates with Public Housing Authorities.

We ran a functional analysis of both systems and compared outcomes against Chicago, Maricopa County, Miami-Dade County, and Denver. Two reforms came out of it. A proactive engagement program to reach at-risk individuals earlier in the process, and centralized oversight of HMIS data to make records consistent across participating agencies.

Placement delays trace to coordination gaps between agencies rather than to capacity alone.

Spring 2025 San Gabriel Valley Council of Governments Cost review of the Jump Start Bus Rapid Transit projects
AreaTransit

SGVCOG needed updated cost estimates for the Jump Start BRT projects, the near-term dedicated bus lanes, signal priority upgrades, and shelters identified in its February 2024 Transit Feasibility Study and slated for delivery before the 2028 Olympics.

We rebuilt the estimate from the feasibility study, benchmarked it against comparable California BRT projects, broke costs out by segment, and adjusted for inflation. The revised figures came in materially above the original. The work was carried into conversations with SGVCOG and Metro.

Projected costs ran 26 to 31 percent above the feasibility study's estimate, indicating a need to revisit bids and contingencies.

Spring 2026 Claremont Homeless Advocacy Program Structuring a relaunched street outreach program
AreaHomelessness

CHAP closed in 2020. Its board asked us how to bring it back, and what the new organization should actually do.

We interviewed leadership at three California homelessness organizations: a community navigator at Tri-City Mental Health, the current and founding leaders of a Santa Cruz volunteer organization, and the President and CEO of Inland Valley Hope Partners. The resulting board memo covered referral partnerships, volunteer recruitment and dispatch, which unhoused populations to prioritize, and the operational steps toward nonprofit status.

Volunteer street outreach fills a gap other regional providers do not cover. Referrals should come through professional case managers.

Students

Joining Path

Path is open to undergraduates at all of the Claremont Colleges. We recruit at the start of each semester. No prior consulting or policy experience is required, and members come from a range of majors.

Members are placed on a project team of four to six students and work on one client engagement for the semester. Teams meet weekly. Expect roughly four to six hours a week, more in the weeks around a deliverable. You will do primary research, conduct interviews with practitioners and public officials, write and edit the deliverable, and in most cases present it to the client.

Team leads are drawn from returning members. Leadership positions open each spring.

Client Subject
Los Angeles County Development Authority Continuum of Care and Coordinated Access System evaluation

LACDA asked us to evaluate the systems that move people from the street into housing across Los Angeles County, with attention to coordination with Public Housing Authorities.

We compared outcomes against Chicago, Maricopa County, Miami-Dade County, and Denver, and proposed a proactive engagement program plus centralized HMIS oversight.

Placement delays trace to coordination gaps between agencies rather than to capacity alone.

San Gabriel Valley Council of Governments Cost review of the Jump Start Bus Rapid Transit projects

SGVCOG needed updated costs for the dedicated bus lanes, signal priority upgrades, and shelters slated for delivery before the 2028 Olympics.

We rebuilt the estimate from the feasibility study, benchmarked it against comparable California BRT projects, broke costs out by segment, and adjusted for inflation.

Projected costs ran 26 to 31 percent above the original estimate, indicating a need to revisit bids and contingencies.

Claremont Mayor Sal Medina Tiny homes as transitional housing

Homelessness in Claremont had roughly doubled since 2020 against limited shelter capacity. The Mayor asked whether a tiny homes village was realistic for the city.

We delivered a research report, a public-facing article, and a funding roadmap, drawing on interviews with unhoused residents, cost analysis, and programs in Tacoma and Santa Clara.

Comparable programs moved up to 40 percent of residents into permanent housing. Homekey and HHAP are the viable funding routes.

About Path

Who we are

Path is a student-run consulting group at Claremont McKenna College. We work with city governments, regional agencies, school districts, elected officials, and nonprofits, mostly in Los Angeles County and the San Gabriel Valley.

Teams of four to six students are supervised by the club's leadership throughout the semester. We do not do legal or engineering work and we are not a substitute for licensed professional services. If you are unsure whether something fits, ask.

What we deliver
  • Research reports and policy memos
  • Comparative studies across jurisdictions
  • Program and cost evaluations
  • Tracking spreadsheets and databases
  • Council, board, and public-facing materials
Why Path

What a partnership gives you

Work you have deferred

Most of what we take on is the research that keeps sliding down the list. A comparison of how peer jurisdictions handled a problem. A cost estimate nobody has had time to check.

No cost and no paperwork

Our work is volunteer. There is no contract to negotiate, no invoice, and nothing to route through procurement.

Minimal demand on staff

An hour at the start to scope the question and an hour at the end to walk through the findings. Anything beyond that is up to you.

An outside look

Students bring no institutional history and no stake in the answer, which makes them useful on questions where internal assumptions have gone unexamined.

A defined end date

Engagements run the length of a semester and finish. You know what you are getting and when.

Documented and sourced

Deliverables are written to be handed to a council or a board, with the underlying research documented so your staff can verify it.

Process

How an engagement runs

Scoping

We meet with your staff before the semester begins, define the question, and agree on the deliverable. If a project is not a good fit for a student team, we will say so.

The semester

A team is assigned and works through primary research, interviews, and analysis on an agreed check-in schedule. Your staff can be as involved as is useful.

Delivery

You receive the finished product before the term ends, along with a walkthrough or a presentation to your council or board if you want one.

About Path

Student-run. Locally focused.

Path partners with city governments, regional agencies, school districts, elected officials, and nonprofits. Our clients are the people who run the places we live in, and our work goes to councils and boards that act on it.

Every engagement is volunteer and free of charge. Teams of four to six students take on one client for the semester, meet weekly, and deliver a finished product before the term ends.

Why join

What Path offers

Work that gets used

Our deliverables go to city councils, school boards, and nonprofit boards. Past memos have been shared with a member of Congress and carried into conversations with Metro.

Access to practitioners

Members interview mayors, agency directors, nonprofit executives, and county staff as a normal part of the work. You will be on those calls, not reading notes from them.

Research and analysis skills

Comparative policy research, cost modeling, program evaluation, and writing for an audience that has to make a decision. These transfer directly to law, policy, finance, and consulting.

Small teams, real ownership

Four to six people per client means no one is carrying a token role. First-semester members write sections of the final deliverable and present to clients.

A path into leadership

Team leads are drawn from returning members. Leadership positions open each spring and carry responsibility for client relationships and team management.

Across the consortium

We recruit from all five Claremont Colleges and teams mix majors. Government, economics, and data science are common, but the work rewards people who write well from anywhere.

The work

What a semester looks like

Every team runs the same arc, scoped with the client before the term begins and delivered before it ends.

Scoping

The team meets the client, defines the question, and agrees on what the deliverable will be. Getting this right is most of the job.

Research

Primary interviews, public records, comparative jurisdictions, budget and program data. You gather what nobody has assembled yet.

Analysis

Cost review, program evaluation, benchmarking against peer agencies, or whatever the question actually requires.

Delivery

A written product the client can hand to a council or a board, and in most cases a presentation to go with it.

Membership

Becoming a Path member

Training

New members are brought through the research methods, interview practice, and writing standards we hold deliverables to before their first client meeting.

Leadership

After a semester, members can apply to lead a team. Leads own the client relationship, set the work plan, and manage four to five consultants with support from the executive board.

The community

Path is a group of people who care about how local government actually works. Members come from across the consortium and stay connected well past graduation.

Claremont Mayor Pro Tem Corey Calaycay Uncontested elections after district voting reform

Claremont wanted to know whether the shift from at-large to district-based voting, adopted to comply with the California Voting Rights Act, was reducing the number of contested races.

We built a comparative dataset of 21 small California cities and tracked outcomes before and after each transition, then delivered options on candidate recruitment and filing.

Contested races fell more than 20 percent. Appointments to vacant seats more than doubled.

Claremont Unified School District Why a special election cost twice its estimate

The district's 2023 special election cost nearly double its estimate. Board President Kathryn Dunn asked why, and whether any of it could be recovered.

We examined California election law, county reimbursement mechanisms, and comparable districts. Our memo set out legislative options and was shared with Representative Judy Chu's office.

Cost underestimation is structural, not local. Small districts face the highest barriers to recovering funds.

Touchstone Mental Health Zoning and community barriers to supportive housing

Local opposition had stalled Touchstone's Lincoln on the Creek project in Edina, Minnesota. They asked how stigma and zoning restrictions block supportive housing statewide.

We delivered a comparative analysis of legislation similar to Minnesota's SF-1370 alongside outreach materials, a virtual facilities tour proposal, and an interview series with residents.

A legislative comparison and a public engagement package built for use in front of a planning commission.

Claremont Homeless Advocacy Program Structuring a relaunched street outreach program

CHAP closed in 2020. Its board asked how to bring it back and what the new organization should do.

We interviewed leadership at three California homelessness organizations and delivered a board memo on referral partnerships, volunteer dispatch, target populations, and nonprofit status.

Volunteer street outreach fills a gap other regional providers do not cover. Referrals should come through professional case managers.
